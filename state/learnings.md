# What works on Jury Juice (the bot keeps this up to date from Buffer insights)

Last updated: 2026-10-18 (Sunday review; 37 measured posts, Oct 5 to 17; IG reach still tiny everywhere)
Engagement rate = (reactions + comments + shares + saves) / reach (FB: impressions).

## Do more
- Real police court cases told as ONE clear single-photo story. The three biggest posts ever are all this:
  Plate Run / Kansas v. Glover (FB 269k impressions, 922 comments, 20 shares), Brake Light / Heien
  (17k, 75 comments), Kept Waiting / Rodriguez (4.6k, 2.6%, 8 shares). Arrested for Filming (Glik)
  also hit 2.0% FB / 2.7% IG with 8 shares.
- Police topics overall: 14 FB posts avg 0.61% vs county 0.06% (6 posts) and HOA 0.43% (5).
- Single photo posts: FB avg 0.56% over 17 vs 0.20% for 18 tweet-style posts; IG 0.93% vs 0.32%.
- Everyday stops people can picture themselves in (a plate check, a brake light, being kept waiting):
  the comment-heavy posts all ask "could this happen to me?".
- Luke 2026-10-16: police car lights / an officer in the photo. Only 2 measured `policeLights` posts so far
  (FB 0.15%, IG 1.34%): keep using them, not enough data to judge yet.

## Do less
- Carousels: 7 measured, FB 0.25% / IG 0.27% avg vs singles 0.41% / 0.72%.
- County / city stories: 6 measured, FB 0.06% avg, 0 comments. Keep them at ~20% (Luke's split) but pick
  ones with a clear personal stake (a fine, a home, a yard) and a strong photo.
- Quick questions (engage): 10 measured, FB 0.24% avg, ~350 impressions each. Keep to 1 a day max.
- Quirky non-US / novelty topics (pigeons, guinea pigs, weevils, monkey selfie): ~0%.
- Long, detailed captions or source lists.

## Test next
- Caption style: payoff 3 measured (FB 0.10%, IG 0.17%) vs classic 3 (FB 0.41%, IG 1.52%). Far too early;
  keep 50/50 until each has 10+.
- Photo look: blue 1, panel 1, original 15. No verdict yet.
- Police posts on stops and searches of ordinary drivers (the Glover/Heien/Rodriguez family) vs police
  history/trivia (First Police Car: FB 27 impressions).
- Closing questions that invite personal experience ("has this happened to you?") vs "was the court right?".
